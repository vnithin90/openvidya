[Module 1](/electricity/what-is-current) answered *what current is*: the rate at which
charge crosses a surface,

$$
I = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}.
$$

This module asks a different question: **what sets that rate** inside a simple model of
a metal wire?

## The product model

In a steady, uniform wire with one kind of mobile carrier,

$$
I = n\, q\, A\, v_d
$$

| Symbol | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| \(n\) | how many mobile carriers per cubic metre |
| \(q\) | charge on each carrier (here \(e\)) |
| \(A\) | cross-sectional area of the wire |
| \(v_d\) | **net drift** speed of the carrier population along the wire |

So current is large when any of these is large. **Speed alone does not tell the story.**

## Same current, different mixes

<M2Twin client:visible />

If current were “just how fast charges move,” two wires with the same \(I\) would need
the same drift speed. They do not. A thicker wire or a denser carrier gas can carry the
same current with a **smaller** \(v_d\).

## How small is drift speed?

<M2DriftEstimate client:visible />

Household currents are amperes; drift speeds in this model are often **millimetres per
second or less**. That is not a paradox: \(n\) is enormous, so even a crawl of the
*net* population moves a lot of charge per second.

Remember: the number \(n\) you type is a **model input** (order-of-magnitude for copper),
not a measurement performed on this page.

## What this module does not tell you

- What electrons are doing in detail (thermal motion + small drift) — **Module 3**
- Where the electric field comes from in a circuit
- Energy, power, or whether current is “used up”
- Quantum structure of real metals

The classical glide picture is a **model**. Module 3 exists because that model is easy
to over-believe.
