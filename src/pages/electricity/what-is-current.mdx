This module has **one job**: separate two questions students often glue together.

- *How much charge moved?*
- *How quickly did it move?*

The answer to one does **not** give you the other. Everything else about electricity —
what carries the charge, how fast carriers go, what they travel through — is left out
on purpose. Those are later modules.

## Charge can be counted

<Scene1Counting client:visible />

A particle that *has* charge carries it in one fixed size, written $e$ — the
**elementary charge**, and $e$ is a positive number. But charge comes in **two kinds**.
A proton carries $+e$. An electron carries $-e$. Same size, opposite sign.

So a large charge is not a large particle. It is a large number of particles — and,
because the two kinds cancel, what counts is the *difference* between them:

$$
Q = N e, \qquad N = N_+ - N_-
$$

Add one of each and nothing changes. Balance them exactly and the object is
**neutral** — which does not mean empty. A neutral object is full of charged
particles; it simply has as many of one kind as the other.

Then switch to the log axis for the huge numbers: each step is ×1000, because you
cannot draw $10^{20}$ dots honestly.

## Charge can cross a surface

<Scene2Surface client:visible />

Send a **packet** of charge past two counting places. The dashed lines are not doors —
they are places *you* choose to stand and count, like counting people walking past a
mark on the floor.

Move place B. Both counters should agree on how much was in the packet (if the whole
packet went past). You changed **where you counted**, not the charge itself.

The two readouts (coulombs and number of elementary charges) are the same quantity in
two units, because $Q = Ne$.

One thing this scene deliberately does **not** say: which *kind* of charge is in the
packet. Whether the movers are positive or negative — and which way each would go —
decides the *direction* we assign to a current, and that is a later module. Here we
are only counting how much crossed.

## How long did it take?

<Scene3Rate client:visible />

This is the point of the module. **Run A** and **Run B** transfer the **same** charge
($10\,\mathrm{C}$). Only the time is different.

Watch the **clocks** and the **bars**. Both bars end at the same height. One clock
finishes sooner. That larger “charge per second” is a different quantity from the charge
itself.

$$
I = \frac{\Delta Q}{\Delta t}
$$

After you have seen the comparison, the page names it: **electric current**.

## How much, and how fast

<ChargeRatePanel client:visible />

A AA cell can transfer **more total charge** over its life than a lightning stroke, while
the stroke has a **far larger rate**. Both statements can be true. That is the module in
one picture: amount and rate are different questions.

## What this module does not tell you

It says nothing about what carries the charge, how fast those carriers move, what they
move through, or what happens when they arrive.

In particular: **this page has not claimed how fast charge carriers move.** If you
picture electrons racing through a wire, this module has neither confirmed nor corrected
that picture.
